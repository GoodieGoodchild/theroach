Everyone who visits Knysna says the same thing about the air. It is *soft*. Wet, green, heavy with the forest. Beautiful to walk through.

It is a nightmare to grow in.

## What the coast actually does

The Garden Route sits between the Outeniqua mountains and the sea, and it gets weather from both. Mist rolls off the lagoon most mornings and sits low until the sun burns through — if it burns through. Rain arrives without asking. Humidity that would be a dream in a dry inland summer becomes, in a dense flower in late season, an invitation.

Anything that traps moisture and holds it there is a problem. Density is a problem. Poor airflow is a problem. A grower who packs plants close because more plants sounds like more of a good thing is asking for trouble in about week six of flower.

## Why so much coastal weed is rushed

Here is the honest reason a lot of Western Cape dagga gets pulled early, dried fast and moved quickly: because the longer it sits, the more the climate has a chance to get at it.

Rushing solves a real problem. It just creates a different one. Anything dried in a hurry keeps the chlorophyll in — that harsh green edge in the throat, the taste people assume is just what it tastes like. It isn't. That's a shortcut you're tasting.

## Doing it the slow way anyway

We cure slowly in a climate that fights slow curing. That means the work is mostly not the plant at all:

- Space between plants, at a cost in yield we simply accept
- Air moving constantly, especially at night when the temperature drops and the humidity climbs
- Defoliating with intent, so light and air reach the middle of a plant rather than only its outside
- Watching, daily, at the point in flower when a warm wet week can undo four months

The plant is the easy part. The environment is the whole job.

## Fifteen years in one valley

There is no shortcut for knowing what one particular piece of ground does in February and what it does in July. When the mist lifts. Which corner stays damp two days longer than the rest of the plot. Where the wind actually goes.

That knowledge does not transfer. Move this operation somewhere easier and you would get a different result, probably a worse one, because the whole method is shaped around a difficult place.

Garden Route grown, in every sense that matters.
